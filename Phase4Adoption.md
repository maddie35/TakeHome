# Phase 4: Adoption

Start with shared goals, to help users and to help them speed up content production. Then, finding the easiest, most effective way to do that; adoption comes from making the standard the easiest path that benefits everyone.

I'd then pilot this with 1-2 teams with the most pain points (e.g., their docs generate the most support tickets, or their pages have the worst retrieval). After that, when speaking with other teams, the conversation starts from evidence and first-hand accounts of the benefit.

For teams that remain resistant, I want to understand why their resistance. The most common reason I'd assume is disagreement over standards not fitting specific content. I would go read their pages, ask how they're affected by this, and either fix the standard or, if absolutely needed, allow for an exception. But ultimately, it's about working with them and listening to their concerns or hesitations; that type of conversation will not only give me more insight, but it usually produces a better standard while getting the team on board in the process.

If the standard genuinely fits and the team still resists after conversations, I'd keep publishing and sharing the checker results for their section, highlighting the user impact each time. At a higher level, I'd also regularly share the improvements we're measuring to give everyone insight into the impact. Visible, specific, unarguable data does most of the work. Escalation is something I'd do only when there's a real cost to not adopting, like a broken cross-product experience or an accessibility failure.
